A LIFE OF PURPOSE

A boy from Rameswaram who inspired a nation.

Dr. Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam rose from a modest childhood in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, to become one of India's most respected scientists and its 11th President.

Known for his contributions to India's space and missile programmes, he believed that science, education and imagination could help build a stronger nation. Yet, beyond his achievements, it was his humility and love for students that made him unforgettable.

THE BEGINNING

Small beginnings.
Extraordinary dreams.

Born on 15 October 1931, Kalam grew up in a family in Rameswaram, a coastal town in Tamil Nadu. His early life taught him discipline, simplicity and the value of hard work.

He studied physics at St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, before pursuing aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology. Fascinated by flight and technology, he chose a path that would shape India's scientific future.

Although he did not achieve his initial ambition of becoming an Air Force pilot, he continued forward in aerospace research and found another way to serve his country.

THE MILESTONES

A journey that changed India.

From aerospace research to the highest constitutional office, every chapter reflected a commitment to learning and national development.

EDUCATION

Discovering the world of science

He studied physics at St. Joseph's College and later aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology.

1958
FIRST STEPS

Beginning his scientific career

Kalam began his professional journey at the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO), contributing to India's aerospace research.

1969
SPACE RESEARCH

Joining India's space programme

He moved to the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) and became project director of the Satellite Launch Vehicle programme.

1980
A NATIONAL MILESTONE

SLV-III places Rohini in orbit

Under his project leadership, India's SLV-III successfully placed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit, a major achievement in the country's launch-vehicle programme.

1980s
MISSILE DEVELOPMENT

Advancing indigenous technology

Kalam played a leading role in India's Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, associated with systems including Agni and Prithvi.

1992–99
SCIENTIFIC LEADERSHIP

Advising India's defence leadership

He served as Scientific Adviser to the Defence Minister and Secretary of DRDO. He was also associated with the scientific leadership surrounding India's 1998 Pokhran-II nuclear tests.

1997
THE HIGHEST HONOUR

Receiving the Bharat Ratna

India awarded Kalam its highest civilian honour in recognition of his contributions to scientific research and national development.

2002–07
PUBLIC SERVICE

The People's President

Kalam served as India's 11th President from 25 July 2002 to 25 July 2007. His accessible manner and connection with young people earned him widespread affection.

2007–15
THE TEACHER

Inspiring the next generation

After his presidency, he continued teaching, speaking and writing, encouraging students to pursue knowledge and contribute to society.

2015
A LASTING LEGACY

A final lecture to students

On 27 July 2015, Kalam collapsed while delivering a lecture at the Indian Institute of Management Shillong and passed away. He remained engaged in teaching until the end.
